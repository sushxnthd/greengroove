PROJECT OVERVIEW

A smart-shopping system that moves checkout intelligence from the counter to the shelf.

Green Groove is a student-built IoT retail concept that links a shopper identity, shelf-level sensing and a continuously updated digital cart.

Instead of rescanning every product at the end of a trip, the system is designed to associate product movement with the shopper while shopping is happening.

01 / THE PROBLEM

Checkout repeats work the store has already seen happen.

In a conventional supermarket, every product is physically selected once and then identified again at checkout. That creates queues, concentrates work at the counter and separates billing from the inventory events happening on the shelf.

01

End-of-trip rescanning

Billing starts only after the customer has finished shopping.

02

Weak shopper-item linkage

The system does not know which shopper picked which item until checkout.

03

Inventory lag

Physical shelf changes and transaction state are handled as separate events.

03ENGINEERING DECISIONS✦
DESIGNING THE SYSTEM

The challenge was not one device. It was keeping physical and digital state aligned.

A

Persistent identity

The shopper needs one identity that remains stable across the entire trip, rather than being reconstructed at checkout.

B

Sensing at the source

Product movement is most useful when captured where it happens: at the shelf, not later at a counter.

C

Continuous cart state

The virtual cart becomes a live system state rather than a receipt assembled only at the end.

D

Return-aware logic

A useful system must treat putting an item back as an inverse event, not only detect removals.

WHAT THIS TAUGHT ME

Good product design can be a systems problem.

Green Groove pushed the project beyond interface design. It required thinking about sensing, identity, failure modes, state transitions and how hardware decisions shape the user experience. The main lesson was that a polished interaction only works when the underlying physical and digital states stay consistent.
